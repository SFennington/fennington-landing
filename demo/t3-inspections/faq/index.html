Questions & answers

Home inspection FAQ.

Quick answers about booking, reports, pricing and radon. Don’t see your question? Call (413) 282-8616.

Booking & reports

How soon will I get my inspection report?

Within 24 hours, guaranteed. Most reports are emailed the same day as the inspection. Radon results are ready the same day the 48-hour test ends.

How do I schedule an inspection?

Book online any time on the scheduling page, where you can see Ted’s live availability. You can also call (413) 282-8616 during business hours.

Can my real estate agent get the confirmation too?

Yes. When you book online, add your agent’s name, email and phone number. Adding their email sends them the confirmation automatically.

What information do I need when I book?

The full property address including the town, a unit number for condos, the style and square footage of the home, whether there’s a basement, and your agent’s contact details if you have one.

How long does a home inspection take?

Plan on about 2 hours for homes under 2,400 sq ft, about 2½ hours up to 3,400 sq ft, and about 3 hours for larger homes and multi-family properties.

Do the utilities need to be on?

Yes, please make sure they are. If the utilities are off, some systems can’t be tested and the inspection will be limited.

Pricing & services

How much does a home inspection cost?

Single-family homes and condos start at $550 for homes under 2,400 sq ft. See full pricing for larger homes, multi-family properties and add-on tests.

Do you inspect multi-family homes?

Yes. Two, three and four-unit buildings can be booked online, starting at $600. For five or more units, call for a quote.

What add-on tests do you offer?

Radon gas in air ($175), radon in water ($65), basic water quality ($175 or $250 depending on the lab), sewer line inspection ($275) and termite inspection (from $100, priced by county). Add them when you book.

What about homes over 4,500 sq ft or commercial properties?

Call Ted at (413) 282-8616 for a quote.

Radon & well water

How does radon testing fit with my inspection?

Ted sets up a digital continuous radon monitor that runs for 48 hours and takes a reading every hour. He needs access to the home 72 hours before or 48 hours after the inspection to set up or pick up the monitor.

What radon level is a concern?

The EPA recommends fixing a home at 4.0 pCi/L or higher, and considering a fix between 2.0 and 4.0 pCi/L. Read more on the radon page.

Should I test the well water?

If the home is on a private well, a basic water quality test and a radon-in-water test are both recommended. Add either one when you book.
