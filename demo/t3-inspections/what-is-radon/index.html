Radon testing

What is radon gas?

A colorless, odorless gas that can build up in homes. Testing is the only way to know the level.

What it is

Radon is a colorless, odorless, radioactive gas. It forms naturally from the decay (breaking down) of radioactive elements, such as uranium, found in different amounts in soil and rock throughout the world. Radon in the soil and rock can move into the air and into underground water and surface water.

Why it matters

Radon is the second leading cause of lung cancer, after smoking. It’s measured in picocuries per liter of air (pCi/L), and the higher the level, the greater the health risk.

Under 2.0Lower risk
2.0 – 3.9Consider fixing
4.0 and upEPA recommends fixing
02.04.0pCi/L

The US EPA and Surgeon General strongly recommend taking action when a home tests at 4.0 pCi/L or higher. Levels below 4.0 still pose some risk and in many cases can be reduced. The average indoor level is about 1.3 pCi/L, and outdoor air is about 0.4 pCi/L. Even homes with very high levels can be brought below 4.0, and many to 2.0 or less.

Learn more at epa.gov/radon.
